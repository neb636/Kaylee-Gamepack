# Thailand: Mall Day at ICONSIAM (plan)

Written 2026-10-20, revised 2026-10-21 from Dad's photos. Thailand's second family trip. Mommy, Daddy and Kaylee
spend a day at a Bangkok mall: they play at HarborLand and then eat at The Pizza Company. It's built in 3D in the house's look (toon bands, ink outlines, blob
shadows) and reuses the boat trip's trip kit. Read it with `../thailand.md`, `boat-trip.md` (the trip kit),
`../ACTIVITY-TEMPLATE.md` and `docs/play-design.md`.

**One-line pitch:** Mommy drives the silver rental car to ICONSIAM by the river. Kaylee puts on her HarborLand socks
and plays her way round a giant indoor playground: she's a vet in Harbor Town, gives Daddy a crazy haircut, wins the
racing slide, climbs the Big Frame and slides down a dragon, until she's hungry. Then she shares a stretchy-cheese
pizza fairly. The two slices left over go home to Agong in the box, and the big cousins finally look up from their
phones.

## What's really there (so the game feels like the place)

**MEGA HarborLand ICONSIAM** (6th floor, next to the cinema; opened 23 Dec 2021; for ages about 5 months to 15 years):
- **The theme:** "Little Thailand: have fun in one place, travel all over Thailand" (สนุกที่เดียว เที่ยวได้ทั่วไทย).
- **Named rides:**
  - the **Dragon Spiral Tower Slide**, twin dragons round a spiral tower
  - the **Boomerang Donut Slide**, where you ride a ring that swings up and back
  - the **Ta-Khon Roller Slide**, named after Phi Ta Khon, Loei's masked ghost festival, and HarborLand's longest roller slide
  - a low **zip line**
  - **Interactive Ball Attack** and the **Interactive Cannon Target**
- **Thai decor:** Lopburi monkeys, Phi Ta Khon figures, and a **Golden Mountain** to climb. The Golden Mountain is most likely Wat
  Saket's Golden Mount; that's my guess, not a source.
- **Also:** multi-level climbing nets, a ball pool, a bicycle machine where parents race their kids, and the Sky Pod (a wall of
  light-up buttons with a score).
- **Zones:** Toys Room and Little Ville for the small ones, Kids Island (101-140 cm), Junior Jungle (120 cm and up), JumpZ
  trampolines next door, a whole floor of inflatables, and a Kids Cinema with ball pits.
- **The way in:** shoes go on free racks. **HarborLand grip socks are a must** for everyone (฿60), and kids are
  **measured at the counter** because the price depends on height. Play lasts 3 hours, and grown-ups come in on a parent ticket.

### The mini games (Dad picks the order; ★ = my first three)

Each follows `../ACTIVITY-TEMPLATE.md` in short: one core verb that deepens three times, about 20-40 touches and
1.5-3 minutes, at most one or two spoken lines before her hands are busy, and no failing.

#### ★ 1. The Vet Clinic 🩺 (`vet`; caring, body parts)

From Dad's photos: blue scrubs and cap, a plush pony and a golden retriever on the table, a tray of vet tools.

- **Pitch:** a puppy limps in with a sore paw. Dr. Kaylee (in her scrubs and cap) makes every patient better.
- **The toy:** grab a tool from the tray and use it on the patient. It works right where she holds it:
  - the stethoscope: thump-thump hearts float up, loudest over the chest
  - the thermometer: the patient's cheeks glow red, then cool
  - tweezers, cream and bandages: she drags a bandage round and it wraps
  - the brush: the fur fluffs up
- **How it deepens:**
  1. **The puppy's check-up:** the stethoscope, the thermometer, then the sore paw: pull out the thorn, cream, bandage.
  2. **The muddy pony:** bath time. Soap suds appear where she rubs, the shower rinses them, the towel dries, the
     brush fluffs the mane, and a bow goes on top.
  3. **A baby elephant** (Thailand's animal) with a sniffly trunk: medicine on a spoon, a giant tissue. The sneeze blows
     the suds everywhere, then the elephant gives her a happy trunk shower.
- **Her choices:** the bandage's color, the bow, which patient comes in next.
- **Pokeables:**
  - a cat in its carrier that meows back
  - a parrot that copies her sounds
  - the x-ray box (the puppy's bones wag)
  - the treat jar
  - the scale (the patient sits and the needle wiggles)
- **Silly:** the stethoscope on Daddy (BA-BOOM, he hams it up); the thermometer in the pony's ear.
- **Payoff:** each healed patient hops off the table and joins a happy row in the waiting room, wagging.
- **Tech:** a 3D exam room in the house's look, with the tools as 3D props dragged by the finger (like the airport's
  suitcase). Tap zones on the patient: head, chest, paw, tail. New toy animals: a puppy and a pony (`characters/looks.ts`
  has no dog or horse yet); the elephant exists. **Riskiest:** a tool landing on the right part of a 3D animal, every
  time, on an iPhone too.

#### ★ 2. The Barber Salon 💇 (`barber`; silly, shapes)

- **Pitch:** Daddy wants a new look before lunch.
- **The toy:** tools on Daddy's mane:
  - the grow tonic: squirt it and the mane grows long and wild
  - the scissors: snip where she drags, and tufts fly
  - the comb: drag to make spikes, or smooth it down
  - the dryer: big poof
  - color spray: rainbow stripes
  - clips and bows
- **How it deepens:**
  1. Grow and snip. Anything goes.
  2. Colors and the dryer.
  3. A kid customer shows a picture card (spiky, pigtails, curly). Whatever she makes, she gets a laugh and
     "close enough!".
- **Payoff:** the chair spins round to the mirror for the reveal. Daddy loves it and poses, and Mommy takes a photo.
  **He keeps the hairdo for the rest of the trip**, at the pizza table and at home, where Agong laughs (param `hair`).
- **Silly:**
  - snip his horn and it boings back
  - tonic on his eyebrows
  - the dryer on full blows everyone's mane
- **Tech:** the mane as a grid of strands (one instanced mesh), each strand with its own length and color; the
  scissors cut the strands under her finger. **Riskiest:** strands that read as hair in the toon look.

#### ★ 3. The Racing Slide 🏁 (`race`; counting down, taking turns)

From Dad's photo: four wavy orange lanes into pink and white balls.

- **The toy:** climb the steps (tap, tap), sit at the top in lane 1 with Daddy, Mommy and two kids beside her.
  3-2-1, and she taps to scoot faster over each bump. Whee into the balls.
- **How it deepens:**
  1. The first race.
  2. Tap just as she hits a bump and she flies higher.
  3. Race in rubber rings, spinning.
- **Payoff:** a winner's ribbon per race (she always wins by a nose). Daddy lands in the balls head first.
- (A race is also the boat trip's big ride. This one is a quick playground race: no laps, no items.)

#### 4. Cafe & Cake ☕ (`cafe`; colors, mixing, Thai drinks)

From Dad's photos: a white-tiled kitchen, a coffee machine, a blender, breads in baskets, a tea set, a cake stand.

  2. Orders in picture bubbles ("pink!": strawberry and banana).
  3. Thai drinks: **cha yen** (orange Thai tea poured over ice with milk), a coconut shake. Two orders at once.

#### 6. Little Land: giant foam blocks 🧱 (`blocks`; shapes, balance)

From Dad's photo: walls of big foam bricks. Filled in with `../ACTIVITY-TEMPLATE.md` on 2026-10-21, before the greybox.

**One-line pitch:** Daddy bets she can't build higher than him; Kaylee stacks giant foam bricks to build a tower, a
chedi and an elephant, and knocks them down.

**The toy (core verb):** grab a big foam brick and put it down where she wants it.
- How it feels: the brick hangs just above her finger, and a soft outline shows where it will land. It's pulled to the
  nearest spot, drops in with a whump, squashes and wobbles, and the whole tower sways a little and settles. A brick
  half over the edge sways a lot ("whoa!") but stays. A swipe through the tower knocks it down: the bricks fly and
  bounce on the mats, then hop back into the box one by one, up a scale.
- Why it's fun with no goal: building tall and knocking it down is the oldest toy there is.
- Greybox test: the hall's bricks (same size, `BLOCKS.brick`, same five foam colors) on a mat, the brick box, Kaylee,
  Daddy. Does dragging a brick feel good on the iPad, and does it land where she meant, on an iPhone too?

**How the verb deepens (3 steps, same verb):**
1. **Free stacking:** anything goes. After six bricks the tower glows: push it over (tap it or swipe through it).
2. **A picture card to copy:** the easel shows a chedi (a stepped tower with a gold spire), then an elephant. Outlines
   on the mat show where, row by row, and a brick near the picture goes to the next outline. Done: the chedi's gold
   spire hops on top with a temple bell; the elephant gets its face, trunk and ears, trumpets, then shakes itself
   apart.
3. **Taller than Daddy:** Daddy stands beside the stack with a ribbon at his horn's height. When the tower gets past
   it he looks up, ah... ah... CHOO, and blows it down. He laughs; she can keep building, and he sneezes again.
Side verb: none (pushing the tower over is the same brick in her hand, the other way).

**Her choices:** where each brick goes and how tall, which color brick she takes (she can pick any loose one off the
top), when to knock it down, and which way it falls.

**Pokeables (5+ things that react and aren't the answer):**
- the foam wall at the back (the brick she taps pops out and plays a note)
- the big foam ball (it bounces and rolls)
- the foam rocking elephant (it rocks and squeaks)
- the easel (it spins and shows the card again)
- the stars on top of the easel (they twinkle up a scale)
- Daddy (a hop and a laugh) and Kaylee (a hop and a giggle)
- a brick under the others (it boings and the tower wiggles)

**Silly moments (instead of failure):**
- let go over Daddy's head: he wears the brick as a hat, wobbles, and it slides off
- let go over the box: it drops back in, fwip
- let go over the ball: it bounces away
- a brick half over the edge: a big wobble, "whoa!"
- knock it down any time, in any round: crash, everything bounces, and it all comes home to the box

**Story frame (at most 2 lines before her hands are busy):** Daddy: "Can you build higher than me, Kaylee?" / Sparkle:
"Grab a brick!" (with the full loop; the greybox has no voices).
When Sparkle talks during play: when the card first shows ("Copy the picture!"), when Daddy stands up ("Taller than
Daddy!"), and after ~8 s with nothing touched (the 👆 shows the box or the next outline).

**What it teaches, by doing:** balance (a brick on top of two holds; half over the edge wobbles), shapes from a picture
(the chedi's steps get smaller as they go up; the elephant's legs, body and head), and taller and shorter (the
ribbon at Daddy's height). Tap-to-hear facts (full loop): a chedi is a Thai temple tower, shaped like a bell with a
gold spire; elephants are Thailand's animal.

**Payoff (visible, builds up while she plays):** a gold star on the easel for each round (three), the chedi's spire,
the elephant coming alive, Daddy's sneeze. The 🧱 sticker goes on her sock band after the first knock-down.

**Replay:** free building is different every time; the cards; Daddy sneezes at every tall tower; after the third
round it stays open as a toy (and the 🛝 takes her back to the hall).

**Numbers:** about 30-40 touches: round 1 about 8-12 (six bricks or more, the push), round 2 about 13 (six bricks
each card, the easel), round 3 about 5-10 (four rows or more), plus pokes. About 2-2.5 min. No lines before the first
touch in the greybox; one or two with the full loop.

**Sections and state:** one section, `blocks?round=1|2|3&card=chedi|elephant` (`round` 1 free stacking, 2 the picture
cards, 3 taller than Daddy; `card` is round 2's picture, so the elephant has its own link). The stars already on the
easel follow the round. Reads `socks` (her grip socks) and `games`; a link past round 1 has the 🧱 sticker.

**Tech:** a 3D side view (like the vet and the cafe) in the house's toon look. Stacking on a grid with no physics
engine (`grid.ts`, pure, shared with the flat version): the floor is cut into half-brick columns, a brick covers two,
and it rests on whatever is highest under it. Every frame the spots she could put a brick on are projected to the
screen; the one nearest the brick in her hand wins (`spotAt`), with a pull, so it lands where she meant on any screen.
The tower's sway is one spring (each brick leans by its height), and the crash is per-brick arcs with bounces on the
mats. Taps alone play it: a tap on the box hops the next brick to a good spot (the next outline, the top of the
tower), and a tap on the glowing tower pushes it over. **Riskiest:** a brick landing where her finger meant, every
time, on an iPhone too.
