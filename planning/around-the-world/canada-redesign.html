My first pick is Roxy. You already like the camp opening, the 3D set exists, and one mitten can test the strongest clue from Koh Samet. Does Kaylee make the face happen twice?

One winter, six friends

Keep Maple’s invitation and the six-stop map. Give every visit a moment when Maple sees what this particular friend can bring to the team. Let Kaylee cause that moment, then recognise it again when Gus’s geese come down the ice.

The empty bench

Open on Gus passing to five blue scarves. Maple winds up, looks for someone to pass to, and finds an empty bench. She packs six pink scarves into her balloon basket. The map opens on that image.

A place on the team

All six friends are reachable in any order. Each takes a scarf. Back on the map, the new recruit briefly shows their move beside the Ottawa rink. A child returning mid-story can see who has joined.

The last pass

Keep the three-goal game. On the last play, Roxy strips the puck, Ollie spins it across and Misty breaks through the goose wall. The puck comes back to Maple for Kaylee’s shot. Share the Cup’s maple taffy with Gus, then call the shell’s Canada Champion ceremony.

How your observations shaped these choices

Wardrobe and Harborland salon. You see her return to both. In the wardrobe she puts a thing on someone and gets a pose. In the salon she works a tool on Dad’s actual mane and sees the result in the mirror. That points toward a real work surface, permission to mess about and a look she can keep. Misty gets all three.

Koh Samet. Your most specific observation was that she laughs at Dad’s tickled face and comments on it again when she replays. Give Maple and Ollie expressions she can summon, interrupt and repeat with her own hand. A single funny shot at the end of a film would lose that control.

Dumpling House, the pizzeria and Koko’s forest. Food she shapes reaches someone who eats it. A leaf brought to Koko gets a response. Each Canada toy gives her a recipient she can watch immediately: a kit mimics her, Misty tries on the print, Maple catches the photo.

Ice hop, the condor, penguins, river and Venice. The clearest signal is the handoff during ice hop. You’ve also seen less interest in the other four. Difficulty, repetition and unclear instructions are possibilities there. That argues for testing whether she starts independently, especially before building more steering or timing challenges.

Québec · Mont-Sainte-Anne

Roxy’s shadow tent

Move a prop through the lantern light. 1½–2½ min · 20–30 touches

In the current game

The paw stealing marshmallows is a great opening. Then the film hands off to layered 2D forest art. Progress runs through eye pairs, sound sources and a trail of prints. The best character, Roxy, spends much of that time out of reach.

The change

Keep the theft and follow the paw straight onto the glowing tent. Use the film’s 3D clearing as the play space. Retire the required forest search, positional sound hunt and star count. The roasting stick can stay by the fire as scenery.

From first touch to team scarf

“Someone took my marshmallow!” A striped paw appears as a shadow. Maple lifts the tent flap. Three kits are already waiting for a show.

  1. Make it wiggleA mitten puppet is already in the beam. Drag it and the mitten on the canvas waves along. One kit waves back. Moving it over a kit’s ears makes the kit duck, peek, then copy the pose.
  2. Make it enormousThe lamp, a broad tabletop and the canvas are all visible at once. Slide the prop toward the lamp and its shadow grows. Toward the tent it shrinks. The smallest kit spreads its arms until it topples onto its tail.
  3. Catch Roxy in the actMove a round practice puck into the beam. Roxy’s paw darts across the canvas and takes the real puck from its stand. She peeps over the tent with it between her teeth. Present it again anywhere along the beam and she plays along.

The moment to play twice

Put the mitten over Maple’s head. She looks up with just her eyes, crosses them as the thumb waggles, then tries to keep a solemn captain’s face. Moving the mitten away resets her. Kaylee controls the second laugh.

What she learns

She can make the same mitten fill the tent or fit between a kit’s ears by changing its distance from the lamp. Roxy’s nimble front paws also explain how she can steal a puck. A frog peep and owl call are optional discoveries at the edge of camp.

Back in Ottawa

After the first playful steal, Roxy holds out the puck for another go. A pink scarf beside her invites her to join. The three kits become rink-side fans and raise their mittens when Roxy steals from a goose.

Choices, controls and prototype notes
What she chooses
Mittens, a wooden spoon or the puck. Choose the shadow’s size, the kit to tease and how often to repeat a face.
When she gets stuck
A tap on a prop puts it into the beam. Tapping the long table moves it in depth. After eight idle seconds, a kit nudges the prop and the shadow swells. The puck rolls within reach as soon as she has tried changing a shadow.
How to build it
Share the clearing, lantern positions, tent and character rigs with the opening film. Fix the camera at kit-eye height with a clear view of the lamp, prop and canvas. Project a simple silhouette onto the tent for a crisp edge on iPad. Ground every paw, log and tent peg on the same floor.
What could go wrong
Can she tell what is casting the shadow? Prototype the lamp, one mitten, one kit and the giant-face gag first. If she only pats the canvas, let the shadow itself drag the prop. If she keeps looking for animals, bring Roxy into the light sooner.
British Columbia · Great Bear Rainforest

Misty’s sweater shop

Press an inky stamp onto a sweater. 1½–3 min · 20–35 touches

In the current game

Salmon Leap asks for three fish home, then four, then four more before the eggs. Taps already help fish advance. Making the jump easier still leaves eleven fish doing the same job. Misty’s biggest swing happens in the closing film.

The change

Retire the 2D fish-launching game from the map. Give its existing rainforest stop to Misty. Keep the swimming salmon, cedar setting, berry muzzle and soaring pinecone as things she can see or set off herself.

From first touch to team scarf

“Me? On a team?” Misty holds Maple’s tiny sweater against her belly. It barely covers her nose. A bear-sized blank and a wet leaf stamp are waiting on the bench.

  1. Print a crestLift the stamp from the pad and put it anywhere on the cloth. It compresses, lifts with a peel and leaves exactly the shape she chose. One red maple leaf is enough for Misty to try the sweater on.
  2. Make the look hersA salmon and a bear-paw stamp join the leaf. Overlap colours, print a crooked school up a sleeve or stamp twice in the same spot. Misty turns her arm so Kaylee can see the marks she just made. A damp sponge lifts any mark she changes her mind about.
  3. Try Misty’s big pressMisty offers her huge paw as the final stamp. Press it onto the hem. THUMP. A pinecone bounces off the bench. Misty whacks it over a cedar with her stick and then looks down, startled by her own strength. A second press sends another cone.

The moment to play twice

Aim the ink pad at Misty’s nose. Her eyes follow it inward. She sneezes a pink pawprint onto Maple’s spare bib, then holds her nose out for another. On replay the last sweater is already on the hook and a fresh blank sits beside it.

What she learns

Match the leaf stamp to the red maple leaf on the little Canadian flag above the bench. The fish stamp comes from a salmon she can see swimming upstream in the river behind Misty. Touch the river and watch a fish reach the gravel. Its life cycle can stay in the animal book.

Back in Ottawa

Misty pulls on the actual print, adds the pink scarf and joins. She wears it on the map and at the face-off. When the goose wall rises, the same paw wind-up precedes her slap shot. Kaylee can recognise her player across the ice.

Choices, controls and prototype notes
What she chooses
Pick plum, red or pink ink, choose a motif, put it on the chest or either sleeve, erase it or keep the lopsided version.
When she gets stuck
A tap selects a stamp and a second tap places it, including over the edges of the cloth. After idle time, Misty presses one corner as a demonstration. The join scarf appears after her first big press. There is no design to match and no minimum number of prints.
How to build it
Set the bench on the bank of the existing 3D river. Bake a small set of placed marks into one sweater texture shared by the bear in the shop, on the map and at the rink. Store the motif, colour and position locally so a replay or a direct link can reconstruct the look.
What could go wrong
Start with one bear, one stamp and one sleeve. If the mark disappears under her finger or reads as a button press, lift the stamp above contact and slow the reveal. If she dresses Misty and ignores the river, leave fish as an optional discovery.
British Columbia · Tofino

Ollie’s rink-side postcard

Frame a moment with Ollie’s instant camera. 1½–2½ min · 20–30 touches

In the current game

The trip already has a purpose at first. She finds sleepy Ollie and tows her home. After that, steering toward successive spouts and the orca encounter keeps the boat busy, but Maple still needs Ollie for exactly the same reason as when they left.

The change

Keep the pink boat, kelp, harbour, whale performances and underwater sounds. Put Ollie beside Maple from the opening. Replace the compulsory tow and spout tour with one errand Ollie cares about. She wants a picture of her home at the rink before her first away game.

From first touch to team scarf

“Ottawa’s so far from my whales. Can we take them in a picture?” Ollie offers a camera. A humpback lifts its tail behind her and she misses it completely.

  1. Frame a friendThe boat coasts to a stop at a lookout. Drag the big camera over Ollie, a gull or the cove. The view moves under its frame. Release to take the picture. A real-looking print curls out and Ollie spins it straight into Maple’s mitt.
  2. Find a tailprintTwo humpbacks surface in their own time at a distance. Hold the camera toward either one and it lingers through a dive. A white notch on one dark tail and pale speckles on the other show in the prints. Set pictures side by side and spot the same visitor again.
  3. Make the postcardFrame the same visitor wide with the island, or tight on its tail. Ollie noses into a corner and tries to smile with all her teeth. Move the camera back over her and she does it again. Pin any print you like into Maple’s open postcard wallet.

The moment to play twice

A gull-only photo is good mail. So is half of Ollie’s whiskered face. Kaylee can replace the card with a funny outtake and see exactly that composition displayed at the rink on her next visit.

What she learns

She uses the shape and markings on the underside of a humpback’s tail to recognise an individual, the same clue used by whale researchers in B.C. The camera brings the view closer while the boat stays at its lookout. Listen underwater from the rail whenever she wants.

Back in Ottawa

Pinning a picture after the first print completes Ollie’s errand. She wraps her scarf, spin-passes the postcard to Maple and comes to Ottawa. Mount the chosen image on the rink boards. When Ollie whirls a loose puck back, use the same spin and the same soft whip sound.

Choices, controls and prototype notes
What she chooses
Subject, framing and which photo goes to Ottawa. Open-water steering can live behind the boat wheel on replay. No species checklist or perfect shutter moment.
When she gets stuck
A tap on any visible subject frames it and makes a picture. Holding the frame lets a whole surfacing play out. If she pans into empty sea, a spout rises in view after a short pause. Ollie will pass even a picture of water.
How to build it
Reuse the coast, boat, surfacing animation and animal calls. Build a stable viewfinder with two visibly different humpback tails. Save a compact recipe for the postcard (subject, pose, crop and tail pattern) and redraw it for the boards. Only the game world is photographed.
What could go wrong
A camera could become another collection chore. Try outtakes and the instant print-pass before building a second whale. If she photographs Ollie over and over, let that be the whole errand. If she abandons the camera, test passing a floating practice puck with Ollie on the same cove set.

Build order and playtest

Take one toy through a ten-minute playtest before starting the next. Keep the other two as pitches until Kaylee has shown which parts she wants again.

First · Roxy

Give her the iPad just as the paw disappears. Watch what she reaches for, whether she changes the shadow’s size, and whether she brings the mitten back to Maple’s face.

Go on when she can make and repeat a surprise herself. If she waits for another film, fix the handoff from the paw to the first prop.

Second · Misty

Offer one blank sweater and let her decide when it is finished. On the map, watch for recognition of the look she made. On her next visit, see whether she keeps it or tries another.

Go on when she experiments beyond the first print and can put the sweater on Misty unaided. If she just wants to dress the bear, put the sweater on Misty earlier and print straight onto it.

Third · Ollie

Give her the camera with a whale and Ollie in the same view. Does she decide what goes on the card? When she sees it in Ottawa, does she recognise the shot or try to take a different one?

Go on when she chooses her own subject and sends a print without an adult steering. If she waits for a “correct” whale, accept and celebrate her first outtake in front of her.

The country-level test. Give her the familiar map again on another day. Watch which friend she revisits after earning the stamp, whether she anticipates a teammate’s move in the match, and where she hands the iPad back. A voluntary replay or an attempt to show you the joke matters more here than getting all six stamps. When diary access is available, compare repeat starts, exits and hints by section across comparable weeks.

Implementation, accessibility and existing saves
One branch of work at a time
For each future toy, prototype the risky interaction, robot-play it, review a short reel and hear every proposed line in its real voice. Put a bright, voiced build in front of Kaylee before polishing the rest. Record the felt peel, shutter, sliding print and fabric rustle with ElevenLabs as each scene is built.
First touch and free play
One short friend line and at most one spoken instruction, both skippable. Let her play as the line lands. Place the scarf or wallet in reach as soon as its story beat is demonstrated. She can linger or return through the map or shelf. Touch counts and durations above are capacity for a normal visit, never unlock requirements.
Controls and staging
Single-pointer play everywhere, a tap or click alternative for each drag, a replayable spoken prompt and touch/mouse wording via onDevice. Aim for 88 px targets on iPad and at least 64 px on phone. Check the tent face, both sleeves and the camera frame in tall and wide layouts. Start hints around eight idle seconds.
Reuse the existing flow
Keep chapter IDs campfire, salmon and whales, their sticker and stamp IDs, and the six-recruit gate in Place.tsx and story/chapters.ts. Continue through the shared stamp flow and onWin(). Shorten the films around the playable reveal so it happens once. Replays open straight into the toy.
Old links and saved progress
Keep chapter and film URLs working. Map old eyes/sounds sections to the lantern, bandit to the steal, falls/bears to the print bench, spouts to tail portraits and orcas to the postcard. Give every remaining old section a sensible entry, including eggs, stars and listen. Legacy stamps still recruit the right teammate. Use a default sweater and Tofino print for old saves, then replace them when she makes her own.
Integrate and check
Share character looks and scene builders with the films. Change titles, shelf art, map pins, prompts and book encounters together. Deep-link each new beat with sensible defaults. Test a fresh start, partially and fully stamped saves, out-of-order play, an interrupted visit and an edited keepsake on replay. Run the repo’s verify checks and review the result on iPad, iPhone and Mac.
