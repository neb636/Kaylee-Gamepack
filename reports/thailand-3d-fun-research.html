🐘 Making Thailand Roblox-Level Fun

TL;DR

The five things to take away.

1. Be a place, not a quiz

Roblox is a pile of tiny worlds you live in. Thailand's 3D hub should be somewhere she wants to run around, with things to poke everywhere, before any task appears.

2. Make her avatar hers

Avatar self-expression is a core Roblox pull. Let Kaylee dress her own character (and Sparkle) and see it in the 3D world.

3. Collect and grow

Grow a Garden, Adopt Me and Toca all run on tiny loops (plant, water, pet, collect) that pay off visibly. Great fit for markets, food stalls and elephants.

4. Physics = silly = replay

Wobbly ragdolls, splashes, bouncy things and slapstick turn failing into laughing. This matches your "no failing, ever" rule.

5. Other people, even fake ones

The social side is the biggest Roblox draw but you cannot add strangers. Use a living cast (family, animals) plus a way to share with Dad on the same iPad.

Why kids prefer Roblox

What the sources say, and what is my inference. The research I could find is mostly blogs, so treat the claims as directional.

self-expression

Avatars

A University of Washington study of kids 8–13 on Roblox, Minecraft and Fortnite looks at why kids customize avatars, and notes it likely matters most for younger users who are still forming identity.

Obbies and unlocks

Short, simple loops

Analysis of Grow a Garden says it set the template: short sessions, simple mechanics, daily return hooks. A child-media group noted its low-stress, non-violent style is where kids hang out.

Make your own stuff

Unlike fixed-story games, kids build and decorate their own worlds, then invite friends over to see them. Note that Toca Boca World works the same way: drag-and-drop, no goals, nothing locked.

Together

Kids love teaming up and showing off creations. That is also the main thing you cannot copy safely, so substitute: NPC family, shared-iPad co-play, or "show Dad" moments.

caution

What not to copy

Blog sources flag monetization pressure, trading scams and event-driven FOMO. Take the joy, skip the hooks. Toca Boca's "no clear stopping point" is also a parent concern; your 3–6 minute stage structure is a feature.

Ideas for Thailand

Mapped onto what is already in the repo: Agong's shophouse hub, a family roster, src/world/places/thailand/, and Thai culture. Each is a candidate for the "one activity per PR, toy first" process.

Roblox: Adopt Me

🐘 Elephant care and ride

Feed bananas, hose them down (Songkran-style splashing), brush, then climb up and ride along a jungle path. Core verb: care. Deepens: new foods, a baby elephant, then riding.

Roblox: Grow a Garden

🌺 Garden & market stall

Plant mango, jasmine, lotus. Harvest and set them on the shophouse stall; family members walk up to buy. Learning: counting, colors, Thai fruit names.

Roblox: Dress to Impress

👗 Festival dress-up

Dress her avatar (and Sparkle) for Loy Krathong or Songkran. Strut a runway on the shophouse street; the family cheers. No timer, no voting.

Wobbly Life jobs

🍜 Street-food cook

Orders from family: toss noodles, choose toppings, sizzle, serve. Count ingredients out loud. Sloppy physics are funny, never wrong.

Brookhaven / Sago Boats

🛶 Floating-market boat

Steer a boat between stalls, catch fruit tossed from baskets, and deliver. Different places for a different camera (see Controls).

Odyssey capture

🛺 Tuk-tuk ride

Hop into the tuk-tuk (or Kirby-style become it), zoom around town, honk at animals, pick up family and deliver them to the temple.

Obby, gentle

🏯 Temple stepping-stones

Hop across lotus pads and stone steps to the golden pagoda. Falling = a splash and a "plop" back at the last pad, with a giggling fish.

Toca / Adopt Me houses

🏠 Decorate the shophouse

Place lanterns, flowers, and shop signs anywhere in the 3D hub. Her decoration stays between visits (useSaved).

Adopt Me hatch

🥚 Hidden-creature hunt

Find geckos, cats and fish hiding around the shophouse; collect them in a sticker book. Nobody can be missed; the hub always shows a sparkle hint after a while.

Hub-level ideas (apply everywhere)

  • Everything is poke-able. Every prop reacts to a tap with a sound plus a squash/bounce; odd actions get silly responses.
  • A collection that pays out. Coins (or jasmine petals) fill a jar on the shelf; when full the family throws a mini party.
  • Emote wheel. Three big buttons (wave, dance, hop) that her avatar performs and the family copies.
  • Living cast. NPC family wander and react to her by name. This is the safe replacement for multiplayer.
  • Co-play with Dad. A "Dad button" that drops a second character he steers with the other thumb. Roblox kids love playing together; this is the closest safe substitute.
  • Secrets. Tapping things in a pattern or visiting corners reveals surprise hidden spots, like Roblox easter eggs.

Controls for a 5-year-old on an iPad

My design judgment, not research: my searches found no usability study comparing joysticks and tap-to-move for this age.

  • Tap-to-go first. Tap a spot or object and she walks there. A virtual joystick is what Roblox does, so offer it as an optional "grown-up" mode and watch how she does with it.
  • Big, thumb-reachable targets (≥ 88 px per your rules), and auto-jump over small steps so she never needs a jump button.
  • Camera does the work. Fixed or gently following camera; no two-finger orbiting. Keep one camera per scene as the AGENTS rules say.
  • Edges are soft. Invisible bumpers, no falling off the world; if she goes somewhere odd she gets a silly reaction.
  • Let her steer one thing. The fun verbs here (steer, pour, ride) work best when her finger touches the real thing.
  • Test on the iPad in Safari. Keep a flat fallback without WebGL2 as the repo already does.
Easy research win: next time she plays Roblox, record her screen. Count: what she touches in the first 30 seconds, whether she uses joystick or tap, how often she repeats something, and what makes her laugh. That tells you more than any article.

Suggested next steps

  1. Play 20 minutes together of Adopt Me and Dress To Impress. Write down the 3 things she does first.
  2. Pick one core verb from the Thailand ideas (my vote: elephant care or garden & market, as they combine nurturing, collecting and visible growth).
  3. Fill the activity template (planning/around-the-world/ACTIVITY-TEMPLATE.md) and build a greybox toy first; test it on the iPad before art.
  4. Add a hub layer: poke-able props, a coin jar, an emote wheel, and the family wandering. This is the Roblox "world" feel and benefits every later activity.
  5. Add her avatar in 3D with dress-up persisted via useSaved.
  6. Only then add stamps, a finale and the trophy flow.
